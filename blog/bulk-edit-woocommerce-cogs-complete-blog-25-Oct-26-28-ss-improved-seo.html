Need to bulk edit WooCommerce COGS after a supplier changes prices? Bulk COGS Editor for WooCommerce lets you filter products, preview new costs, and apply updates in background batches without changing selling prices.

Watch the Bulk COGS Editor video walkthrough — See how to review product costs and follow the plugin’s workflow before trying it on your own store.

New to product cost tracking? Read the official WooCommerce Cost of Goods Sold guide to enable native COGS and understand product and variation costs before using the bulk editor.

Bulk edit WooCommerce product costs

Step 1 — Open the bulk editor

Go to Products → Bulk COGS Editor and choose Bulk edit. Choose products, set a cost action, and review the changes before you confirm.

Choose a cost action to suit your update. These examples start with a $10.00 cost; check the preview before applying your changes.

Step 2 — Find products and choose an adjustment

Set your filters and click Find products. Here, the editor shows 13,687 matches, with 5,000 selected for a 5.5% increase.

Choose Adjust existing cost → Increase → Percentage for this example. Each variation counts as a separate record.

Step 3 — Choose a range and manage columns

Open Select a range to select the first or last number of matching products. Use Manage columns to show the fields you need while reviewing costs.

The example selects the first 5,000 results and shows a cost moving from $10.00 to $10.55. Add an operation note to make the update easier to identify later.

Step 4 — Confirm the bulk cost update

Click Review 5,000 changes to open the confirmation dialog. Check the action, selected total, and review coverage before clicking Update 5000 products.

Review coverage is separate from selection: this dialog shows 10 reviewed products among 5,000 selected. Other selected products are calculated during processing. Stopping later will keep changes already saved.

Step 5 — Follow background progress

The progress panel shows updates, skips, failures, and time estimates. This example is halfway through: 2,500 of 5,000 products processed.

Updates use Action Scheduler. You can close the browser and return later, provided background workers are running on your site.

Step 6 — Review a completed bulk update

Expand Recent activity → View details to see the completed result. This screenshot records 5,000 updated, no skips or failures, and 00:10:54 elapsed time.

This is one recorded operation, not a guaranteed processing time. Different screenshots can show separate runs and product values; use them as workflow examples.

Export costs and prepare a spreadsheet

Step 7 — Choose CSV export and its columns

Open CSV import / export and select Export CSV. Choose the output columns, then use the product filters to define your export.

Step 8 — Review selected export products

After finding products, review the export preview and selected total. Here, 10,000 products are selected from 13,687 matches, with the note “10K - CSV Export.”

Click Create CSV when the selection and columns are correct. Exporting reads product data without updating costs.

Step 9 — Wait while the CSV is prepared

The export panel shows progress as it builds your file. Keep this page open until the download is ready; the export needs this page to stay open.

At the start, remaining time can show Calculating…. Use Cancel export if you need to stop preparing the file.

Step 10 — Download the finished CSV

When preparation reaches 100%, click Download CSV. This example shows 10,000 of 10,000 records processed and a recorded elapsed time of 11 seconds.

Exports use CSV format, which opens in Excel. The screenshot’s elapsed time is specific to this export and hosting environment.

Step 11 — Open the exported file

Open the CSV in Excel or another spreadsheet editor. The exported data includes identifiers, current cost, optional price columns, product type, and an empty new_cogs column.

Keep IDs and SKUs unchanged so the updated file continues to identify the intended products.

Step 12 — Fill in new product costs

Enter the costs you want to import in new_cogs. In this example, a current cost of 10.90 becomes 12.12734, calculated using an 11.26% increase.

If you use spreadsheet formulas, replace them with values before importing XLSX. The preview may display rounded amounts according to your store’s precision.

Import WooCommerce COGS from CSV or Excel

Step 13 — Upload your cost file

Choose Import CSV, select your file, and expand File requirements. This workflow accepts CSV or XLSX and provides a downloadable template.

Click Validate file to prepare a preview. The example’s 2 MB upload limit comes from that WordPress installation; your site may show a different limit.

Step 14 — Monitor import validation

Preview preparation runs in the background without changing product costs. This screenshot shows 5,749 rows checked against an approximate total of 9,815.

You can return later to check progress. The row count is an estimate until the file has been read. Use Cancel preparation to stop this stage.

Step 15 — Review the validated import

When the preview is ready, check the old and new costs and each row’s status. Here, 10,000 products are selected and the visible rows show $10.90 → $12.13.

Choose which products to include and add a useful operation note. Validation prepares the update; it does not apply it.

Step 16 — Confirm the import update

Click Review 10,000 changes, then check the File import confirmation. The dialog shows how many products you selected and reviewed.

In this example, 20 selected products have been reviewed. Click Update 10000 products only after checking the file, selection, and proposed costs.

Step 17 — Understand the collection stage

After confirmation, the plugin collects matching products before applying costs. This screenshot shows 8,600 collected and confirms that no costs have changed yet.

Collecting finds the products to update. Costs change in the next stage.

Step 18 — Track the applied import

The import is now updating costs. The example shows 8,212 of 10,000 products processed, with no skips or failures at that point.

You can close the browser and return to the editor. Stop remaining updates requests cancellation but does not restore costs already changed.

Step 19 — Check the final import result

Expand the completed activity to review its totals. This import records 10,000 updated, 0 skipped, 0 failed, and an elapsed time of 00:18:25.

Use the available report controls to inspect issues when needed. Completion time depends on the file, products, server, and background workers.

Review History and roll back eligible changes

Step 20 — Find an earlier operation

Open History to find completed, cancelled, and other recorded operations. Filter by status, operation type, user, or dates, or search by operation ID, note, or product ID.

Click View details beside the operation you want to inspect. Notes help distinguish runs that used similar actions.

Step 21 — Inspect operation details

The selected operation displays its type, status, user, updated count, note, timestamps, and duration. This entry is the completed 10,000-product CSV import.

Check that you have the right operation before you review a rollback.

Step 22 — Review rollback values before confirming

The rollback review compares Current COGS (now) with Rollback to (original). Read the overwrite warning, acknowledge it, and click Confirm rollback when you are ready to restore the saved costs.

The visible rows in this screenshot show equal current and original values, with no displayed difference. Do not assume every rollback row changes the cost. Deleted products cannot be restored.

Step 23 — Follow rollback startup

A separate rollback entry appears in History while processing starts. The progress panel shows 0 of 10,000 products processed, with the remaining time still calculating.

Use the detailed progress panel for the operation total. You can return later to check progress or use Cancel rollback to request a stop.

Step 24 — Check rollback at halfway

Here, rollback has processed 5,000 of 10,000 products. The panel shows 50% progress, no skips or failures, and an estimated remaining time.

Time estimates can change as more batches finish.

Step 25 — Review near-complete rollback progress

The rollback has processed 9,879 of 10,000 products, while the interface displays 98%. Refresh the status and wait for the final result before treating the operation as complete.

A rollback-unavailable notice above the progress panel does not mean this active rollback has failed. Check the operation’s actual status and final counters.

Step 26 — Verify the completed rollback

The completed rollback reports 10,000 updated, no skipped or failed products, and 00:19:34 elapsed time. Review the result and spot-check the products you intended to restore.

Rollback requires retained original cost records. Expired and summary-only operations cannot be restored through this workflow.

Configure Settings and recover a stuck operation

Step 27 — Set retention and rollback behavior

Open Settings to configure history retention and rollback behavior, then click Save settings. This screenshot shows a 30-day retention period and overwrite behavior.

Use Force clear current operation only when an operation is stuck and normal cancellation does not work. It is a recovery control, not a way to undo product costs.

Step 28 — Confirm force clear only when needed

The confirmation explains that force clear removes the saved current-operation and import references and requests cancellation. An active worker may continue briefly, and costs already changed are not restored.

History and rollback data are kept. Check operation history before starting another update. For background-task diagnostics, see the Action Scheduler administration guide.
